PAGE 2 - BRIEF ABOUT THE IDEA
--------------------------------------------------------------------
GoNoGo: know if a product will sell before you stock it.

Small and mid-size retailers launch new products on gut feel. A bad buy
ties up cash in stock that doesn't move. A missed good one goes to a
competitor.

GoNoGo is an AI launch-decision agent. A seller snaps a photo of a product
they're thinking of stocking and enters the cost price. A crew of
specialist AI agents then checks the idea against:
  - the shop's own data in BigQuery: sales history, similar products,
    inventory, customers, budget
  - the live market: Gemini with Google Search, looking at real product
    pages on Myntra, Flipkart, Ajio and Amazon.in

In under a minute, the seller gets Go, No-go or Go with conditions, with
three plain-language reasons. Each reason opens to the numbers or web
pages behind it.

Our core principle: Gemini understands, searches and explains. Every
number comes from SQL or Python, and the verdict comes from the seller's
own rules checked in code. The AI can't invent a figure or change the
decision.


PAGE 3 - OPPORTUNITIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
How is it different from existing ideas?
  - Most retail AI tools are dashboards (they show the past) or demand
    forecasters (one number, no reasons). GoNoGo makes a decision and
    backs every reason with evidence you can click.
  - Generic chatbots guess numbers. In GoNoGo, every figure is calculated
    from the shop's own data, and the verdict is set by rules in code, so
    it's consistent and auditable.
  - It judges a product against this shop: its customers, brand, what it
    never sells, its stock and its budget. It doesn't judge against "the
    market in general".
  - Competitor links are real product pages that Google Search returned,
    not made-up URLs.

How does it solve the problem?
  - It turns a week of spreadsheet work and marketplace browsing into one
    question that takes about a minute.
  - It stops cash going into slow sellers: comparable products' sales
    speed, margin after fees, shipping and returns, and break-even are all
    checked before buying.
  - It protects the brand: a product that doesn't fit (e.g. a brass puja
    diya in a fashion shop) is always a No-go.
  - It keeps experiments affordable: an experiment budget comes from the
    shop's own profit run-rate, and each launch must fit inside it.

USP of the proposed solution
  1. A verdict with evidence, not a chart: every reason links to its data.
  2. Numbers by code, words by AI: no hallucinated figures.
  3. The seller's rules, enforced: margin, break-even, sales speed and
     budget are set on the Business page and saved in BigQuery.
  4. Photo-first: snap a product, and GoNoGo finds it, crops it and
     suggests a selling price from the shop's own similar products.
  5. A visible agent crew (Iris, Scout, Ledger, Echo, Penny, Vera) that
     streams its work live, so the seller sees how the decision was made.


PAGE 4 - LIST OF FEATURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Discovery (the launch check)
  - Start from a photo: Gemini Vision finds every product in the image,
    draws numbered boxes and checks each one for fit with the shop. Pick
    one and it's cropped and evaluated.
  - Suggested selling price from the shop's comparable products (SQL).
  - Chat-style interface: answers stream in live, one line per agent. You
    can stop a run mid-way, and copy or retry an answer.
  - Verdict: Go / No-go / Go with conditions, key numbers (margin,
    expected units per month, break-even weeks, opening order, cash
    needed) and three reasons.
  - References panel: similar products in the shop with their sales, rule
    checks (pass/fail), sales chart, and each agent's evidence.
  - Similar products online: real Myntra / Flipkart / Ajio / Amazon.in
    pages, each with its title, price, picture and rating read from the
    page itself.
  - Recent checks saved in the browser and reopenable from the sidebar.

The agent crew
  Iris  (Product) - What is it? Does it fit the shop?
                    Gemini + photo + shop profile + catalogue match
  Scout (Market)  - Online prices and competitors
                    Gemini + Google Search grounding
  Ledger (Demand) - How fast similar products sell, trend, returns
                    BigQuery
  Echo  (Fit)     - Who buys it, and what they buy with it
                    BigQuery basket + buyer analysis
  Penny (Profit)  - Margin, break-even, opening order, budget
                    Python
  Vera  (Verdict) - Applies the rules, explains the result
                    Rules in code + Gemini explanation

Rules checked
  Hard: fits the shop, minimum margin, maximum break-even, minimum sales
        speed, experiment budget, no dead stock in the same line
  Soft: sales speed near average, category trend, price vs market

Shop pages
  - Business: 12-month KPIs vs the previous 12 (revenue, units, margin,
    orders, AOV, return rate), monthly revenue, category performance,
    channel split, experiment budget, editable rules.
  - Shop profile: who the shop is, its customers, positioning, style and
    what it never sells. The agents judge against this.
  - Products, Inventory, Sales, Returns, Customers, Suppliers: searchable,
    sortable tables. Inventory flags Reorder, Overstock (more than 120
    days of cover) and Dead stock (no sale in 90 days).

Platform
  - Access-code gate for the live demo, mock mode for running offline,
    one failed agent never crashes a request, works at phone width and in
    dark mode.


PAGE 5 - PROCESS FLOW DIAGRAM
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Seller: photo + cost price (or a text description)
        |
        v
Autofill (Gemini Vision) -> finds products, boxes, fit,
                            suggested price (SQL)
        |  seller picks one / edits
        v
Iris - Product agent -> category + "does it fit this shop?"
        |
        +-- doesn't fit -> NO-GO (other agents skipped)
        |
        v  fits - run in parallel
+-----------------+------------------+------------------+
| Scout - Market  | Ledger - Demand  | Echo - Fit       |
| Google Search   | BigQuery sales   | BigQuery baskets |
+--------+--------+--------+---------+--------+---------+
         +-----------------+------------------+
                           v
        Penny - Profit (Python): margin, break-even,
        opening stock, cash, budget
                           v
        Vera - Verdict: rule checks in code
        -> GO / NO-GO / GO WITH CONDITIONS
        Gemini writes headline + 3 reasons (can't change the verdict)
                           v
        Chat answer + References panel (evidence, links, checks)

Use cases:
  1. A buyer checks a new product before ordering.
  2. The owner sets rules and budget on the Business page.
  3. The owner reviews stock problems on the Inventory page.

PAGE 7 - ARCHITECTURE DIAGRAM
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Browser (HTML + CSS + JS, no build step)
   |  HTTPS, NDJSON streaming
   v
Cloud Run (asia-south1) - one FastAPI service: pages + /api/*
   |  access gate (HMAC cookie)
   |
   +-- Orchestrator (asyncio) - runs agents, streams
   |   start/done/failed per agent
   |     +-- Product, Market, Verdict, Autofill
   |     |      -> Vertex AI Gemini 2.5 Flash (us-central1)
   |     |         + Grounding with Google Search
   |     +-- Market -> reads the store pages Search returned
   |     |             (JSON-LD price, og:image)
   |     +-- Demand, Fit, Budget, Pages
   |     |      -> BigQuery (gonogo_shop, asia-south1),
   |     |         parameterised SQL files
   |     +-- Profit, rule checks -> pure Python
   |
   +-- Settings (rules + shop profile) <-> BigQuery settings table

Auth: Application Default Credentials (Cloud Run service account),
      no keys in code
Build/deploy: gcloud run deploy --source -> Cloud Build ->
              Artifact Registry -> Cloud Run


PAGE 8 - TECHNOLOGIES TO BE USED IN THE SOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  - AI: Gemini 2.5 Flash on Vertex AI (google-genai SDK): vision,
    structured JSON output, explanations
  - Grounding with Google Search: live market prices and competitor
    product pages
  - Data: BigQuery: sales, products, inventory, customers, suppliers,
    settings
  - Backend: Python 3.11, FastAPI, asyncio, Pydantic, Uvicorn
  - Hosting: Cloud Run, Cloud Build, Artifact Registry
  - Security: IAM service account with least-privilege roles,
    Application Default Credentials, access-code gate
  - Frontend: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, streaming (NDJSON), inline
    SVG charts, light/dark theme
  - Demo data: Python generator for the synthetic shop "Urban Loom":
    180 products, 6,000 customers, ~53,000 order lines over 24 months

--------------------------------------------------------------------

      Within free tier at demo traffic
  BigQuery (small dataset, a few MB)
      Within free tier (1 TB of queries per month)

      About Rs 1-2 per launch check
  Grounding with Google Search (5 grounded searches per check)
      Free up to the daily quota; beyond it, roughly Rs 15 per check

PAGE 11 - PROTOTYPE PERFORMANCE REPORT / BENCHMARKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured on 18 Oct 2026 on live Gemini and BigQuery with test_local.py.

Test case 1: High-waist active leggings, cost Rs 450, price Rs 1,299
  Verdict:    GO
  Total time: 56.5 s
  Why:        Similar products sell 1.58x the shop average, 44.6%
              margin, break-even in 3 weeks

Test case 2: Wool overcoat, cost Rs 6,500, price Rs 8,999
  Verdict:    NO-GO
  Total time: 44.5 s
  Why:        Sells at 0.07x average (limit 0.7x), 11.7% margin
              (minimum 20%), break-even 36 weeks (limit 12)

Test case 3: Brass puja diya, forced into "T-Shirts"
  Verdict:    NO-GO
  Total time: 20.2 s
  Why:        Product agent caught that it's outside the shop's range;
              the other agents were skipped

Time per agent (leggings run):
  Product (Gemini) ............................. 5.7 s
  Demand (BigQuery) ............................ 1.1 s
  Fit (BigQuery) ............................... 1.0 s
  Market (Gemini + Search, 5 grounded
          searches + page reads) .............. 32.2 s
  Profit (Python) .............................. under 1 ms
  Verdict (Gemini explanation) ................ 12.3 s

What the results show:
  - Accurate: all three cases got the expected verdict, including a
    deliberately mislabelled product.
  - Consistent: verdicts come from rule checks in code, so the same
    inputs always give the same decision.
  - Fast where it counts: BigQuery agents answer in about 1 second.
    Market research is the slowest step, and it runs in parallel with
    the others.
  - Fails safely: a failed agent is marked "failed" and the rest carry
    on. If the fit check can't run, it fails closed.
  - Transparent: the seller watches each agent finish live, so a
    ~45-second answer doesn't feel like waiting.


PAGE 12 - ADDITIONAL DETAILS / FUTURE DEVELOPMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Make it a Go: for a No-go, calculate the smallest change that passes
    every rule (lower cost, a new price, a smaller opening order), then
    re-run with one click.
  - Compare up to 3 ideas: evaluate in parallel and rank them by margin,
    speed, cash and risk.
  - Ask about this verdict: chat with Gemini function calling ("What if
    I sell at Rs 999?"), where every number comes from a calculator tool.
  - Inventory agent: warn about cannibalising existing products and
    overstock in the same category.
  - Connect real shops: Shopify, WooCommerce and marketplace seller APIs
    instead of the demo dataset; multi-shop accounts.
  - Learn from outcomes: track what launched products actually sold and
    tune the rules.
  - Speed: cache market research per product type to bring checks under
    20 seconds.
  - Beyond fashion: the rules and shop profile are generic, so the same
    agent works for electronics, beauty or home stores.

Note: Urban Loom is a synthetic demo shop; no real customer data is used.
